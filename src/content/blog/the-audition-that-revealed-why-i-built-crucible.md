# The Disaster Is Exactly Why I Built Crucible

## I Bombed a Reading For A Friend

This week I was reminded of just how hard it is for me to sound natural. It's frustrating as hell, but it adds so much fuel to me as I build Crucible.

Here's the scene. A friend of mine is an actor. He was submitting an audition video and needed someone to read the other part in the scene. Now, I have zero acting experience beyond a play in elementary school, so I did what seemed reasonable: I Googled it, watched some YouTube videos on what not to do, and figured out the gist. The reader's job is simple in theory: don't be too big, don't be too loud, be in the scene with the actor, and create a space for them to shine.

I ran through the lines on my own beforehand, and felt pretty comfortable.

## Then the Camera Started Rolling

Fast forward to the audition recording, and I fell apart. My tone was either too flat or too big. I was focused so much on how my voice sounded that I couldn't be present in the scene with my friend. My brain was too busy narrating my own performance back to me. In the end, another actor saved the day, my friend recorded an incredible audition, and I can't wait to see him get the part.

But it emphasized something for me that I have clearly been carrying around for a while: I struggle to sound natural, to be in the moment without overthinking the performance. And that is exactly why I built Crucible.

## Same Problem, Different Camera

I don't need to audition for directors, but I do need to build my personal brand. When I launched my consulting brand, every single video I recorded felt contrived. The chemistry I needed with my audience just wasn't there. I knew something had to change. At the same time, I noticed how my clients spoke about their own passions, products, and services in conversation, as well as how they froze when a camera showed up. I realized they were fighting the same battle I was: camera shyness, overthinking every spoken phrase, frozen when they needed to be present in the scene.

This is what Crucible is built to solve. Not "how do I sound polished," but "how do I get people to just be themselves" long enough to capture something real.

## What Being "In the Scene" Actually Looks Like

Here's what I learned about myself since: I am not naturally in the moment for the full recording, and that is fine. I might spend the first thirty seconds thinking about the points I want to make. But once I start going, I can just run with it. Ramble a little bit. Get distracted, regroup, keep going or stop and pick it back up. What matters is that I get the chance to capture all of my thoughts.

And once those thoughts are captured, Crucible handles the second step: rearranging them into something that is still fully my voice, my thoughts, but cleaned up in a way that actually reaches people. Because authentic voices are not automatically viral voices. Even with great ideas, sharp analysis, brilliant whatever, if you do not communicate them clearly, nobody will listen.

You don't need to become a polished performer or outsource your voice to a ghostwriter, or settle for what AI thinks you sound like. Just capture your real, rambling, distracted, honest train of thought out of your head, and then shape it into something people want to devour.

## Forging Ahead

I could not survive one reading without freezing up. But give me a camera, ten minutes, and permission to ramble, and I will hand you a week of content that is still 100% me. That contradiction is exactly why Crucible exists. Brain dump your ideas, let the overthinking happen for the first thirty seconds if it needs to, and then just talk. The forge does the rest. If you have ever listened to yourself on camera and cringed the way I did, you already know why this matters. I still need to develop my on-screen chemistry, but Crucible helps me overcome a big part of my struggle.
